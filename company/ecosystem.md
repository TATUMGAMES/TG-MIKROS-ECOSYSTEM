# Tatum Games Ecosystem

Tatum Games is best understood as a connected system rather than a collection of unrelated products.

## The Problem

Independent studios commonly face several structural disadvantages:

- limited access to comparative data;
- limited internal analytics and data-science resources;
- difficulty interpreting retention, churn, monetization, and player behavior;
- dependence on platform algorithms for discovery;
- limited marketing budgets;
- limited creator and community reach;
- and long production timelines.

Tatum Games addresses those problems across the development lifecycle.

## Why the Ecosystem Matters

The objective is to give developers more of the infrastructure required to build, understand, market, distribute, and improve games.

That is why MIKROS Analytics, MIKROS Marketing, MIKROS Mafia, Orchestra, and Tatum Tech reinforce one another.

## Orchestra's place

Orchestra is the production and creation layer. It is the environment in which an idea becomes a project: research, plan, build, check, repair, and keep the result. The product definition is in [Orchestra](../orchestra/README.md). The system is explained in the [architecture](../orchestra/architecture.md).

The neighboring products do different jobs:

- MIKROS Analytics understands players and performance.
- MIKROS AI explains game data and supports the next decision.
- MIKROS Marketing promotes games and handles discovery campaigns.
- MIKROS Mafia and MIKROS Bot connect community distribution.
- Tatum Tech trains emerging creators through game production.

A lifecycle relationship is not a live integration. The connections that are actually documented are:

- MIKROS Marketing can place a game in Orchestra AI's discovery database, with priority during an active campaign and a listing afterward without that priority.
- Orchestra treats MIKROS MCP as first-party knowledge when it plans MIKROS tool use.

Do not describe the other products as features inside Orchestra, or Orchestra as a control panel for MIKROS.
