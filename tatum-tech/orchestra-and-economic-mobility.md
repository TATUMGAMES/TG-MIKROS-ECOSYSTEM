# Orchestra, Tatum Tech, and Economic Mobility

Orchestra is becoming a core production technology inside Tatum Tech.

## The Production Problem

Traditional game development can require long timelines, specialized technical skills, multiple software tools, and large teams.

For an emerging creator, those barriers can make it difficult to move from:

> I have an idea for a game

to:

> I have a playable, polished, portfolio-ready, or publishable product.

## Orchestra's Role

Orchestra is designed to reduce that gap.

It can help coordinate:

- planning;
- code generation;
- specialized agents;
- project execution;
- image generation;
- video generation;
- audio and music;
- UI/UX;
- game research;
- asset intelligence;
- browser-game generation;
- game-engine workflows;
- validation;
- repair loops;
- and persistent project state.

## Why This Matters for Tatum Tech

The objective is not to replace learning.

The objective is to let participants spend more of their limited program time producing, iterating, testing, and shipping.

A participant who can move from concept to playable output faster has more opportunities to:

- build a portfolio;
- demonstrate skill;
- publish a game;
- form a studio;
- pursue an internship;
- pursue employment;
- test a commercial idea;
- or create an income-generating digital asset.

## October 10, 2026

Participants at the October 10, 2026 Tatum Tech cycle are scheduled to use Orchestra.

This represents an important evolution in the program: AI becomes production infrastructure rather than a standalone classroom topic.

## Relationship to MIKROS

Orchestra helps participants **build**.

MIKROS helps their games **understand players**.

MIKROS Marketing helps their games **reach players**.

MIKROS Mafia helps create **community discovery and distribution**.

Together, the system is designed to support a more complete path from learning to production to market participation.
