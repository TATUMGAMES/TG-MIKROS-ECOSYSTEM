# Tatum Tech

Tatum Tech is Tatum Games' workforce-development and game-production program for youth and young adults.

**Tatum Tech is a program, not an event.**

The program trains emerging creators in videogame development and interactive media through real production cycles.

Participants work in structured teams to build playable or publishable games while gaining experience with industry tools, AI workflows, production methodology, mentorship, portfolio development, testing, distribution, and career pathways.

## Current Program Model

- 16-week production cycle
- 2 cohorts per year
- approximately 20 teams per cohort
- approximately 500–600 participants served annually
- 8-hour Saturday commitment during structured programming
- approximately 80 in-person instructional hours
- approximately 60 independent guided hours through the Tatum Tech mobile platform
- approximately 140 total hours per participant

## Production Milestones

Each cohort moves through:

1. Team Formation and Pre-Production
2. Prototype Development
3. Vertical Slice Completion
4. Full Production
5. Polish and Launch Readiness

## Events Are Part of the Program

Tatum Tech holds large public-facing events aligned with its cohort cycles.

These events provide:

- live playtesting;
- audience feedback;
- community distribution;
- industry exposure;
- demos;
- mentorship;
- speaking engagements;
- and validation of participant projects.

The events are important, but they are not the program itself.

## Why Tatum Tech Is Different

Tatum Tech does not stop at exposure.

Participants build.

The model is production-first and designed to create:

- playable games;
- portfolio-ready work;
- publishing opportunities;
- entrepreneurial opportunities;
- industry connections;
- internships and employment pathways;
- and potential economic mobility.

## Orchestra

Orchestra is becoming a major part of the Tatum Tech production model.

It is designed to help participants move from idea to playable and production-ready game faster by coordinating AI models, agents, tools, assets, project state, and game-development workflows.
